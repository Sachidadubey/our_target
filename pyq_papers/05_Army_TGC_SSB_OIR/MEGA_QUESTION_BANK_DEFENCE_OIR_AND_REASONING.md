## 1. VERBAL INTELLIGENCE RATING TESTS

#### Q1. Syllogistic Deduction
**Statements:** All soldiers are brave. Some engineers are soldiers.  
**Conclusions:**  
I. Some engineers are brave.  
II. All brave persons are soldiers.  
* **Answer:** **Only Conclusion I follows.**  
* **Explanation:** The intersection between engineers and soldiers lies entirely inside the set of brave persons.

#### Q2. Word Relationship Analogy
**Question:** Radar : Detection :: Cryptography : ?  
* (A) Computation  
* (B) Encryption / Security  
* (C) Transmission  
* (D) Hardware  
* **Answer:** **(B) Encryption / Security**  
* **Explanation:** Radar's primary operational function is detection; Cryptography's primary operational function is secure encryption.

#### Q3. Ranking & Order
**Question:** In a row of 35 officer cadets, Vikram ranks 11th from the left end. What is his rank from the right end?  
* (A) 24th  
* (B) 25th  
* (C) 26th  
* (D) 27th  
* **Answer:** **(B) 25th**  
* **Explanation:**  
  $$\text{Total} = \text{Left} + \text{Right} - 1 \implies 35 = 11 + \text{Right} - 1 \implies \text{Right} = 35 - 10 = \mathbf{25th}$$.

#### Q4. Blood Relations (Coded)
**Question:** If $A + B$ means $A$ is the brother of $B$; $A \times B$ means $A$ is the father of $B$; $A - B$ means $A$ is the sister of $B$. Which of the following means $P$ is the nephew of $Q$?  
* (A) $Q + R \times P$  
* (B) $Q - R \times P$ (where $P$ is male)  
* (C) $P + R \times Q$  
* (D) $Q + R - P$  
* **Answer:** **(B) $Q - R \times P$ (with $P$ as male)**  
* **Explanation:** $Q$ is the sister of $R$, and $R$ is the father of $P$. Thus, $P$ is the son of $Q$'s brother/sister, making $P$ the nephew of $Q$.

#### Q5. Letter Coding
**Question:** If `DEFENCE` is coded as `EDGDMBD`, how is `OFFICER` coded?  
* **Answer:** **`PEEJBDQ`**  
* **Explanation:** Pattern is alternate $+1, -1$:  
  $D(+1)=E, E(-1)=D, F(+1)=G, E(-1)=D, N(-1)=M, C(+1)=D, E(-1)=D$. Applying to `OFFICER`:  
  $O(+1)=P, F(-1)=E, F(+1)=G, I(-1)=H, C(+1)=D, E(-1)=D, R(-1)=Q$.

## 2. NON-VERBAL SPATIAL, CUBE & DICE TESTS

#### Q6. Painted Cube Cutting (Extremely Frequent in OIR)
**Question:** A solid wooden cube of side 4 cm is painted red on all six faces. It is then cut into small equal cubes of side 1 cm.  
1. How many total small cubes are formed?  
2. How many small cubes have **exactly 3 faces painted**?  
3. How many small cubes have **exactly 2 faces painted**?  
4. How many small cubes have **exactly 1 face painted**?  
5. How many small cubes have **zero faces painted**?  
* **Detailed Formulas & Solutions ($n = 4 / 1 = 4$):**  
  1. Total cubes = $n^3 = 4^3 = \mathbf{64}$.  
  2. 3 faces painted (Always corner cubes) = $\mathbf{8}$ (for any cube).  
  3. 2 faces painted (Edge cubes) = $12 \times (n - 2) = 12 \times (4 - 2) = 12 \times 2 = \mathbf{24}$.  
  4. 1 face painted (Face center cubes) = $6 \times (n - 2)^2 = 6 \times 2^2 = 6 \times 4 = \mathbf{24}$.  
  5. 0 faces painted (Inner central core) = $(n - 2)^3 = (4 - 2)^3 = 2^3 = \mathbf{8}$.  
  * *Verification:* $8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64$ cubes!

#### Q7. Dice Face Inversion
**Question:** A dice shows three views:  
* View 1: 3, 5, 2  
* View 2: 3, 1, 4  
* View 3: 5, 6, 2  
What number is directly opposite to **3**?  
* **Solution:**  
  In Views 1 & 2, number **3** is adjacent to 5, 2, 1, and 4.  
  Since an adjacent number can never be opposite, the only number that is NOT adjacent to 3 is **6**.  
* **Answer:** **6**.

## 3. PPDT MASTER STORY WRITING & GROUP DISCUSSION TEMPLATES

In Day 1 PPDT, candidates are shown a hazy picture for 30 seconds and given 4 minutes to write a story.

```
THE WINNING PPDT 3-ACT STRUCTURE
├── Act 1 (The Hero & Perception): Introduce a central character (Age 22–24, energetic, positive mood).
├── Act 2 (The Practical Action): Define an actionable challenge (avoid dramatic murder/accident themes).
└── Act 3 (The Concrete Outcome): Detail the organized steps taken and a successful result.
```

#### Master Scenario: Technical Rural Development
* **Image:** A village scene showing two young men near electric poles and open fields.
* **Hero:** Sameer, a 23-year-old computer engineering graduate visiting his ancestral village.
* **Story:**  
  *Sameer noticed that irregular voltage spikes frequently damaged the village tubewell motors during peak irrigation seasons, delaying wheat sowing. He discussed the issue with the local panchayat and visited the sub-station junior engineer to map the grid feeder timings. Using his technical background, Sameer assisted the farmers in installing automatic phase-reversal starter cutoffs and drafted a petition to the electricity board for a dedicated agricultural transformer. He also organized a youth camp to teach basic motor maintenance. Within three weeks, the dedicated line was energized, ensuring uninterrupted water supply for 80 acres of farmland and saving repair costs.*
* **Why This Story Gets You Recommended:**  
  Shows initiative, practical technical leadership, communication skills, constructive attitude, and zero unnecessary melodrama.
